# Positional foundation and foundational core

## Status of the starting point

The library does **not** declare carry as an axiom. Lean's natural numbers are
the inductive type generated by `Nat.zero` and `Nat.succ`; arithmetic
operations such as addition, multiplication, powers, division, and remainder
belong below this project. The object of departure here is simply the ordinary
finite positional representation of those naturals in a base `b > 1`.

We call this layer the **positional foundation**. In narrative prose it may
also be called the **object of departure**. Neither expression denotes a new
kernel axiom.

The formal dependency chain is

$$
\text{Lean type theory}
\to \mathbb N
\to +,\times,\operatorname{pow},\operatorname{div},\operatorname{mod}
\to \text{positional decomposition}
\to \text{saturation and carry}.
$$

The module `CarryGeometry.PositionalFoundation` defines

$$
q_k(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{b^k}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
r_k(n)=n\bmod b^k,
$$

and proves the unique decomposition

$$
n=r_k(n)+b^kq_k(n),\qquad 0\le r_k(n)<b^k.
$$

It then defines saturation of the lowest `k` positions by

$$
r_k(n)=b^k-1
$$

and proves, rather than assumes,

$$
r_k(n)=b^k-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r_k(n+1)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b^k\mid n+1.
$$

The explicit saturated block satisfies

$$
(b^k-1)+1=b^k,
$$

whose quotient is one and residue is zero. This is the formal version of “a
saturated positional block transfers one unit to the next place.”

## Forced normalization

`CarryGeometry.Normalization` distinguishes an unrestricted raw coefficient
from an admissible digit. For natural-number arithmetic, raw coefficients are
modeled in `ℕ`; this already covers the coefficient growth produced by sums,
products, and powers. An extension to signed raw coefficients in `ℤ` would be
relevant to subtraction, but is not needed or claimed by the present theorem.

For `b > 0`, every coefficient has the Euclidean decomposition

$$
a=(a\bmod b)+b\left\lfloor a/b\right\rfloor.
$$

The library proves this is the unique decomposition whose first term lies in
the digit alphabet. After weighting the coefficient by position `j`, the same
identity is

$$
a b^j=(a\bmod b)b^j+\left\lfloor a/b\right\rfloor b^{j+1}.
$$

Therefore normalization does not change value. It redistributes exactly the
part that cannot remain in the finite state space of the current position.
Moreover,

$$
a<b\iff\left\lfloor a/b\right\rfloor=0,
\qquad
b\le a\iff0<\left\lfloor a/b\right\rfloor.
$$

The theorem `finiteAlphabetNormalization_forced` packages existence,
admissibility, overflow, and uniqueness. The word “forced” refers to this
uniqueness under two requirements: preserve the represented value and restore
the current coefficient to the finite alphabet.

The global map `normalizeCoefficients` accepts an arbitrary finite list of
natural coefficients, preserves its weighted value, and produces only digits
below `b`. Already canonical digit lists are fixed points. See
[`FORCED_NORMALIZATION.md`](FORCED_NORMALIZATION.md) for the complete statement
and the sexagesimal examples.

## Finite states and positional compression

For `b > 1`, the repository proves

$$
\#\{\text{admissible strings of length }k\}=b^k
$$

and that positional evaluation bijects these strings with `[0,b^k)`. This
identifies the `Fin (b ^ k)` probability space with the actual finite digit
strings, instead of merely choosing a finite set with the same size.

The same finite alphabet is not closed under unnormalized addition in any
genuine base. For `b > 2`, it is also not closed under unnormalized
multiplication. Addition alone already proves that arithmetic closure and a
finite per-position alphabet require redistribution between scales.

When two windows have different state counts, the smaller residual interval
embeds canonically in the larger one. The corresponding signal map is
extension by zero, conjugated by the two compression amplitudes. Its reverse
is a left inverse, while the opposite composite is projection onto the image.
Strict cardinality inequality implies that the coordinate embedding is not
surjective. Consequently the unequal-window API has the exact finite
partial-isometry behavior without asserting a nonexistent equivalence.

## Mathematical content

For a natural base `b`, depth `k`, and real exponent `sigma`, the public API
uses

$$
\operatorname{carryMass}(b,k)=b^{-k},\qquad
\operatorname{carryAmplitude}(b,k)=b^{-k/2},
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{deformedAmplitude}(b,\sigma,k)=b^{-k\sigma}.
$$

At depth `k`, the residual space is `Fin (b ^ k)`. Every specified class is a
singleton, so its finite uniform probability is `b^{-k}` independently of the
chosen class. In particular, the saturated pre-increment class `b^k - 1` is
the event characterized arithmetically as producing carry after adding one.

For `b > 1` and `k > 0`, quadratic compatibility is rigid:

$$
\operatorname{deformedAmplitude}(b,\sigma,k)^2=b^{-k}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma=\frac12.
$$

The same normal form proves that the global compatibility locus is identical
for every genuine positional base.

## Sharp boundaries

The hypotheses are semantic boundaries, not proof artifacts.

* At `k = 0`, no depth has elapsed and every exponent gives amplitude one.
* At `b = 1`, no positional decay occurs and every exponent is compatible.

Both degeneracies are explicit public theorems. Notice that the arithmetic
decomposition itself only needs `b > 0`; the stronger `b > 1` begins exactly
where decay and uniqueness of the quadratic exponent begin.

## Consolidation theorems

`positionalCarryUniversality` packages the law for an arbitrary residual
class. `arithmeticCarryUniversality` applies the same result to the saturated
class already proved to generate carry. No new mathematical mechanism is
introduced by either theorem.

Running `#print axioms` shows a further separation: the primitive arithmetic
carry theorems in `PositionalFoundation` use only `propext`. The global
finite-list normalization and real-power layers remain inside the project's
standard allowlist `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## Scientific status

This repository is a frozen foundational kernel. New work must identify itself
as one of:

1. consequence of the kernel;
2. application of the kernel;
3. bridge to another formalization;
4. genuinely new mathematical structure.

`CarryGeometry.WindowEquiv` and `CarryGeometry.WeightedTransform` are in the
fourth class and are kept outside the dependency chain of the positional
foundation itself. They package the established finite evaluation bijection,
define matched-window camera changes only under an explicit equality of state
counts, and conjugate reindexing by the already established quadratic
amplitudes. They do not assert a finite equivalence for unequal state counts
or a global all-bases orthogonality theorem.

Green identities, Weyl operators, primes, zeta functions, spectral operators,
and analytic continuation are deliberately outside this repository.

## Lean foundations referenced

The terminology above follows the official Lean documentation:

- [Natural Numbers](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Basic-Types/Natural-Numbers/)
  describes `Nat` as generated logically by `zero` and `succ`, with arithmetic
  operations specified on that model;
- [Inductive Types](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The-Type-System/Inductive-Types/)
  explains that recursors and subsequent properties are derived from the type
  constructor and constructors.

## Provenance

The scalar definitions and rigidity arguments were distilled from the
Apache-2.0 project
[`native-carry-geometry`](https://github.com/thiagomassensini/native-carry-geometry),
at revision `01a536beaa9a045fdf91b511315ab309008ff602` (tag `v0.4.0`), whose
public theorems already contained the complete logical chain. This repository
isolates that chain and adds the explicit arbitrary-residue-class probability
statement and the consolidation theorem.
